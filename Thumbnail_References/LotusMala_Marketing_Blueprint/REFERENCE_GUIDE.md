## 3. Critique of previous work
- **Title/image duplication:** the buyer-question title and 'WHO WOULD BUY THIS?' image largely repeat each other. Let the title name the LotusMala planning exercise and let the image raise one concrete customer question.
- **Hierarchy:** the top topic tag, large multi-line headline, decorative rule and channel footer create several competing levels. Remove nonessential badges and use one dominant focal point plus one short message.
- **Subject selection:** S1 supplies the best authentic human anchor. S2's hidden face should not be forced into a portrait concept; the board can lead that direction.
- **Composition:** large opaque panels reduce the useful photographic area. Favor a photo-led composition or a clearly intentional two-frame editorial composite. Do not fabricate a scene of Bibek holding a board.
- **Readability:** reduce dependence on small handwritten details and the acronym CTA. A large genuine board detail can provide context, while a short label supplies the phone-size message.
- **Light and fidelity:** improve exposure conservatively. Preserve glasses, eyes, skin, expression and handwriting; do not reconstruct missing details or hide changed anatomy behind a crop.
- **Promise:** 'FROM PLAN TO ACTION' may suggest implementation. This recording explains a planned call to action; 'BUYER → CTA' or '6 STEPS' is more precise when the title gives context.

These are editorial observations and risks, not measured explanations of CTR or retention. No audience testing or performance claim is made.

## 5. Proposed directions — not final designs

### A — Person + the buyer question
- **Reason to click:** a specific business question connected to a real person and a named brand.
- **Reference lessons:** A1, A2. One human anchor; short high-contrast question.
- **Source:** a clean original equivalent of S1. A real S2 board detail can be secondary.
- **Composition:** close authentic portrait taking roughly half to two-thirds of the useful visual area; eyes/glasses clear. Board detail stays readable enough to establish the subject, not to carry the entire lesson. A two-frame composite must remain editorial rather than imply an unfilmed pose.
- **Proposed text:** 'WHO IS THE BUYER?' or the shorter 'WHO BUYS?'.
- **Title pairing:** 'I Built a Marketing Blueprint for LotusMala'.
- **Proposed color/type:** natural room/skin colors, charcoal localized scrim, ivory text, one restrained teal accent. Inter Bold; Anton only if a short phrase benefits from condensed type.
- **Tradeoff:** strongest human connection, but a weak portrait cannot be repaired by inventing a sharper face or expression.

### B — The real board + the framework
- **Reason to click:** understand the scope and sequence of this six-part planning exercise.
- **Reference lessons:** B1, B2. An actual artifact demonstrates what is being explained.
- **Source:** clean S2/S3-equivalent whiteboard frames.
- **Composition:** one large original board detail; crop irrelevant frame edges and glare conservatively. Keep actual ink unaltered. A face is optional. Use one short overlay; no decorative fake charts, six invented steps, or redrawn handwriting.
- **Proposed text:** '6 STEPS' or 'BUYER → CTA'.
- **Title pairing:** 'From Buyer Persona to CTA | My LotusMala Blueprint'.
- **Proposed color/type:** genuine off-white board, dark ink, charcoal text/scrim, muted blue accent. Inter Bold/Black; use a large number only when paired with the documented six-part structure.
- **Tradeoff:** subject/process clarity is high; the cover can feel like a generic course if LotusMala and the personal-project context disappear.

### C — My learning project
- **Reason to click:** see a course idea applied to a specific real planning project.
- **Reference lessons:** C1, C2 provisional; C3 as a typography-only fallback.
- **Source:** clean S1-equivalent portrait, or an authentic project/board detail if no portrait is suitable.
- **Composition:** photo-led natural room context, one recognizable face or artifact, modest negative space for a short project label. Preserve the actual speaking expression; no fake surprise, certificate, laptop dashboard or success prop.
- **Proposed text:** 'MY COURSE PROJECT'.
- **Title pairing:** 'My LotusMala Plan: Customers, Search Intent and Action'.
- **Proposed color/type:** natural pale background and skin tones; charcoal/ivory text with a muted warm accent if needed. Inter SemiBold/Bold.
- **Tradeoff:** honest and personal, but less topic-specific. The title must identify the actual LotusMala/customer-journey subject.

These palettes and fonts are proposals, not approved brand rules. The three directions are for this subject; other episodes require a fresh evidence fit.
